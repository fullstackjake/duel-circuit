# Production review: audio (2026-11-20)

A review, not a build: no code or assets changed. Beside it: the interface review (`PRODUCTION-REVIEW-UI-2026-11-20.md`)
and the game design review (`PRODUCTION-REVIEW-GAME-2026-11-20.md`).

**Question from the owner:** how complete and how polished are the game's sound and music, and which changes would
raise them most for the effort?

**The limit, plainly:** nobody has ever listened to this game in a test (every check runs muted), and I can't hear
either. Everything below comes from the code, the sounds rendered to WAV by the harness and measured offline
(length, peak, loudness, spectrum). Anything that turns on how it *sounds* is marked PLAUSIBLE and listed in the
listening checklist (section 7).

## 1. Verdict

The sound is **wider than most fan games and well engineered, but thin where players spend the most time.** Every
sound is synthesized in code (no files, no licence risk, nothing of Konami's). Duels have a cue for nearly every
main event, 17 layered themes react to the duel (the boss's tune with their ace, drive and dread at low LP, a heartbeat
before a lethal attack), bosses get a sting and their own victory and defeat endings, and the prized-card chimes are
even tuned to the music's key. What lets it down is **repetition and gaps**: one 22.9-second menu loop plays on every
screen outside duels (deck editor, shop, campaign, all 227 story scenes) and starts again from the top every time; the
everyday duel theme, about 70% of campaign duels, is a 32.5-second loop with no tune. There's no mix bus (no limiter,
no master volume), the interface volume is tied to the effects volume, pausing a duel swaps to the menu music, and
several duel moments are silent (coin toss, phases, chains building and resolving, LP counting).

**The three changes that would raise it most:**

1. **Music variety where the hours go** (M1 + M2): two or three more menu-side themes (deck editor, shop, story moods)
   and a longer everyday duel theme with a real tune and A/B sections, all in the existing theme system. About 5
   agent-days; needs an owner listening pass.
2. **A proper mix** (Q1, Q2, Q3, M3): an AudioMixer with master, music, effects and interface groups and a limiter;
   interface sounds that don't depend on the effects volume; the duel music kept through a pause; mute in the
   background. About 3 agent-days, checkable without ears.
3. **Fill the silent duel moments and make chains build** (Q4, Q5, M5): a coin toss, phase ticks, LP counting, a
   rising pitch per chain link, a resolve cue, distinct banish and set sounds, a draw-game ending. About 3.5 agent-days.

## 4. Findings, ranked by how much they cost the game's feel

Severity: **High** = heard all the time and wears on every session; **Medium** = a clear gap or fault in a common
moment; **Low** = polish. Status: CONFIRMED (in the code or measured) or PLAUSIBLE (needs ears).

### 1. Outside duels, one 22.9-second loop plays everywhere, from the top each time (High; CONFIRMED)

- `Sfx.Music` has one track, `menu` (`Sfx.cs:73-76`): four chords (Am F C G) at 84 bpm, two bars each, pad, bass and
  an arpeggio, no tune, no drums. Measured: 22.86 s, -21.2 LUFS integrated.
- It plays on every non-duel screen (`DuelController.cs:2360-2363`): title, menus, deck editor, shop, trader, campaign,
  recipe book, grading, all 227 story scenes, the van, versus screens. A 30-minute deck-building session hears it
  about 79 times.
- Each return to a menu starts it again at bar 1: a faded-out track is destroyed (`Sounds.cs:369-373`) and `Switch`
  builds a new one that starts from the top (`Sounds.cs:186`, `NewTrack`).
- Story scenes carry moods in their triggers (`firstloss`, `final`, `finale`, `guest:`, `bracketwin`...;
  `campaign/story.txt` header) but all play the same loop.

### 2. The everyday duel theme is short, has no tune, and carries most duels (High; CONFIRMED, measured)

- `duel` (`Sfx.Themes.cs:66-70`): 16 bars at 118 bpm = **32.5 s**, then exactly the same again. No melody; the lead is a
  quiet chord arpeggio.
- It plays in 58 of the 83 circuit matches (every match but each circuit's last, Gauntlet aside), non-final Tag Cup
  matches, non-boss ladder seats, Library cups, brackets before the final and free duels (`BossMusic.cs`,
  `DuelController.cs:1216-1253`, `DuelController.Brackets.cs:170-175`). A 10-minute duel hears it about 18 times.
- Its one reactive change for an ace, the lead layer, adds **0.2 dB** (bed+drums -23.3 LUFS, with lead -23.1): in
  practice nothing changes when an ace lands (boss themes: +1 to +2.5 dB, Nocturne +6 dB).
- Boss loops are 24-49 s (Gauntlet 24.0 s at 160 bpm, Nyx 49.2 s). Fine for one boss duel; short for a long one.
- `master` is reused for every Library cup's last match, every Every Era round and every bracket final
  (`DuelController.cs:1220,1253`, `Brackets.cs:174`), so it's the second most-heard duel theme.

### 3. Pausing a duel switches to the menu music, and the duel theme restarts (Medium-High; CONFIRMED in code)

- `UpdateMusic` counts `_menu.IsOpen` and `_settings.IsOpen` as "menus" even mid-duel (`DuelController.cs:2360-2363`),
  so opening the pause menu crossfades to the menu loop.
- After a second the duel track is destroyed (`Sounds.cs:369-373`). Closing the menu builds a new one: the theme starts
  from bar 1, and its layers fade back in from zero over a second.
- Players pause to read or change a setting; every pause breaks the duel's mood.

### 4. Several duel moments are silent, and chains don't build (Medium; CONFIRMED)

- **Coin toss:** no sound (`DuelController.Toss.cs`: no `Sounds` call). It's the first thing in every duel.
- **Phase changes:** banner only (`BoardFx.cs` `PhaseBanner`).
- **Chains:** every link plays the same `activate` (`BoardFx.cs:1107`), though `DuelEvent.ChainLink` carries the link
  number. Links in quick succession get *quieter* (repeat fade to 40%, `Sounds.cs:126-129`), the opposite of a build.
  Resolution and chain end are silent.
- **Same sound for different things:** banish and send-to-GY use `destroy` (`BoardFx.cs:305`); a Set uses `place`;
  a Flip Summon uses `summon`; position changes are silent.
- **LP counting:** the counter rolls in silence; `lp_tick` (`Sfx.cs:48`) is never played.
- **Draw game:** plays `turn`, the new-turn chime (`DuelController.cs:5718`): the rarest ending gets the plainest sound.
- **Reaction bubbles and story lines:** no voice of any kind (no VO, no text blips).

### 5. The volume controls are coupled and coarse; there's no master volume (Medium; CONFIRMED)

- `Sounds.Play` returns when Sound effects is 0, *before* it checks the interface flag (`Sounds.cs:123`), and multiplies
  every sound by `EffectsVolume` (`Sounds.cs:139`). So **Sound effects Off silences clicks too**, and at the defaults a
  click plays at 0.8 x 0.8 = 64%. The Interface row says "apart from the duel's sounds" (`MenuScreens.cs:609`), and
  the Sound effects row says it covers "buttons" (`MenuScreens.cs:602`): the two texts disagree with each other.
- "Button clicks and hovers": no hover sound plays anywhere.
- Each volume is a click-to-cycle row, off-20-40-60-80-100, and 100% wraps to Off (`MenuScreens.cs:525`): from 100% the
  next click silences, and 90% or 70% isn't possible. No master volume; no slider.
- The boss victory/defeat endings and the sting follow Music volume (`Sounds.cs:300,352,364`); the everyday `win`/`lose`
  follow Sound effects. A player with music low and effects high hears loud everyday wins and quiet boss wins.

### 6. The game keeps playing sound when it isn't in front (Medium; CONFIRMED)

- `Application.runInBackground = true` (`DuelController.cs:90`), so duels and music carry on behind other windows.
  There's no focus handling for audio (`OnApplicationFocus` doesn't exist in `unity/Assets/Scripts`) and no setting.
  Most games mute or dip when unfocused; on a Mac with a browser in front, the menu loop keeps going.

### 7. No mix bus or limiter; the "voice pool" doesn't limit voices (Medium; CONFIRMED in code, audible PLAUSIBLE)

- No `AudioMixer` anywhere (no `.mixer` asset, no `outputAudioMixerGroup`). Every source sums straight to the output.
- Measured pile-ups at the default 80% effects (the music adds up to about 0.22 more at its default 50%):

| Pile-up (offsets as in code or close) | Sum peak |
|---|---|
| final blow: `damage_big` + `impact` + `win` 0.2 s later | **1.38** (1.73 at 100% effects) |
| ace lands: `summon_big` + `activate` | 0.99 |
| battle: `impact` + `destroy` 0.9 + `damage_big` | 0.85 |
| board wipe: five `destroy` 60 ms apart (repeat fade) | 0.74 |

  Anything over 1.0 clips at the output. Whether the overlaps line up this tightly in a real duel wasn't run.
- The 10 "voices" are round-robin `AudioSource`s played with `PlayOneShot` (`Sounds.cs:133-139`). `PlayOneShot` layers on
  a source; it doesn't stop what's there, so this caps nothing (Unity's 32 real voices do). Worse, `voice.pitch` is set
  on each play: when a voice comes round again, a clip still ringing on it is **re-pitched** by up to ±3%. A prize
  chime (1.0-1.3 s, tuned to the key) can be pushed off key if nine other sounds play during it; `win` (2.1 s) and
  `level_up` (2.0 s) likewise.

### 8. Two special guests have no theme, and the harness's own check fails (Medium; CONFIRMED, run)

- `--boss-music` exits 1: Duchess Ilse and Vera are special guests (`campaign/guests.txt`) without a theme, so
  `BossMusic.ForGuest` falls back to the everyday theme (`BossMusic.cs`, `ForGuest`). Every other guest has their own.
- Their versus screens play no sting either (`BossSting` skips the everyday theme, `DuelController.cs:924`).

### 9. Tonal effects are in fixed keys over music in 17 keys (Medium; CONFIRMED untuned, clash PLAUSIBLE)

- The prize chimes are tuned to the music (`Sounds.cs:136-137`, `KeyRatio`); nothing else is. `summon` is a C major
  chord, `special` D major, `summon_big` C major, `activate` C major (high), `rare` G major, `level_up` C major, `win` a
  C major arpeggio, `lose` a falling C minor line (G F Eb C G) (`Sfx.cs:29-62`).
- Over the everyday theme (E minor, with F# and B in its chords) `lose` puts Eb and F against E and F#: semitone rubs,
  and the music comes back to full under it after 0.8 s while `lose` rings for 2.6 s. Over Gauntlet (F# minor) a
  C major summon is a tritone away. Whether it sounds wrong or just "sound-effecty" needs ears.

### 10. Packs and reveals have two tiers; the duel's best chimes aren't used there (Medium-Low; CONFIRMED)

- Reveal sounds: commons `flip`, Rare/Super `rare`, **every rarity from Ultra up** `burst` (`ShopScreen.cs:1038,1084`).
  Secret, Ultimate, Ghost, Starlight and Quarter Century sound the same as an Ultra.
- The game already has distinct Secret, Ghost and Starlight chimes (`Sfx.Prize.cs`), used only when such a card lands
  in a duel.
- The cards dealing in after the tear are silent; the music doesn't dip under `burst` or `level_up` (only negations,
  big damage, win/lose and prizes duck: `Sounds.cs:43,138`).

### 11. Big hits lose their weight on laptop speakers (Medium-Low; CONFIRMED measured, speaker response PLAUSIBLE)

- The heavy sounds get much of their size from 40-55 Hz sine thuds (`Thud(…, 55)`, `50`, `40`; `Sfx.cs:32,45`,
  `Sfx.Themes.cs:577`). With a 150 Hz high-pass (roughly a laptop speaker), momentary loudness:

| Sound | Full range | Above 150 Hz | Lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| `summon_big` | -10.3 | -16.7 | 6.4 dB |
| `damage_big` | -12.9 | -19.0 | 6.1 dB |
| `impact` | -16.4 | -19.1 | 2.7 dB |
| `damage` | -15.2 | -17.2 | 2.0 dB |
| `destroy` | -15.7 | -15.9 | 0.2 dB |

  On laptop speakers **big damage comes out quieter than ordinary damage** (-19.0 against -17.2). A harmonic layer
  (a distorted copy an octave or two up, 100-300 Hz) would carry the weight on small speakers.

### 12. The synthesis is basic: aliasing, no space, no variation (Low-Medium; CONFIRMED measured, how it reads PLAUSIBLE)

- Square and saw are naive (`Sfx.cs:84-94`): above a few hundred Hz their harmonics fold back as off-pitch tones.
  Measured: `dp` (a 2093 Hz square) has aliases at 147 Hz (-25 dB under the note) and 4333 Hz (-28 dB); `lp_tick` at
  600 Hz (-28 dB). Square leads in the themes are low-passed, which helps.
- No reverb: the only space is a single-tap echo (`Echo`, `Sfx.cs:189-196`). Everything is mono with no panning, so
  your side and theirs sound the same.
- Variation is a random ±3% pitch (`Sounds.cs:137`). Every noise source uses seed 1234 (`Sfx.cs:132`), so each noise
  sound is identical sample for sample every time; there are no alternate takes.
- Effects don't follow the duel speed: at speed 10 the animations run at 0.32 (`DuelController.cs:6122`), the clips at
  full length (summon_big 1.7 s), so fast duels pile sounds up.

### 13. Mix balance: effects sit about 12 dB over the duel music (Low-Medium; CONFIRMED measured, taste PLAUSIBLE)

- Defaults: effects 80%, music 50% (`Sounds.cs:30,49`). A `summon` (-15.6 LUFS momentary) plays near -17.5; the
  everyday duel bed and drums (-23.3 LUFS integrated) near -29.3. Effects-forward is usual in card games; 12 dB may
  leave the music as wallpaper.
- The menu loop is about 2 dB louder than the everyday duel bed at the same setting (-21.2 vs -23.3 LUFS), so entering
  a duel feels like a drop. All the themes' everyday mixes sit within 2 dB of each other (-22.5 to -23.2 with lead),
  which is good.
- The spread between effects is wide but mostly deliberate (summon_big -10.3 down to shuffle -24.0); `turn` (-19.7) is
  close to `draw` and `place`, so the turn change doesn't stand out much.

### 14. The ace's tune enters mid-phrase (Low; PLAUSIBLE)

- Layer gains move linearly over a second whenever the state changes (`Sounds.cs:362-363`), not on a bar line. The
  layers are in sync, so there's no seam, but a melody fading in on beat 3 of bar 11 can sound accidental. A lethal
  drop at 6x speed is meant to be sudden and is fine.

### 15. Docs and licence notes are behind the code (Low; CONFIRMED)

- `docs/DEVELOPING.md` lists "hovers" among the effects; `docs/PLAYING.md` and Settings promise hover sounds.
- `docs/dev/UI-ROADMAP.md` item 22 describes "a tense duel loop" at "1000 LP"; the code has seven layers at 2000 LP.
- `LICENSING.md` has no row for the music and sound: they're written as code in `src/Ygo.Engine/Audio`, so AGPL by the
  `src/` row, but a reader looking for "music" won't find it. If sample-based sound ever ships, it needs its own row
  and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` entries.
- `docs/dev/TODO.md:346` already notes the show's audio has never been heard.

## 5. What already works well (keep it)

- **Everything is made in code.** No files, no licence question, nothing of Konami's or from the official games; a
  modder can't break it by losing an asset. README says so.
- **Layered adaptive themes done carefully:** all layers render to the same length, wrap their tails round the loop
  (no seams; the harness checks it), start on the same DSP sample (`PlayScheduled`, `Sounds.cs:196-205`), and one gain
  keeps every combination under 0.95 (`Sfx.Themes.cs:523-533`).
- **Boss presentation:** a sting on the versus screen (built in a moment, the full theme on a worker thread), each boss
  of a band in their own key, a semitone up each Gauntlet match, and victory and defeat endings built from the boss's
  own tune.
- **Tuned prize chimes:** built from scale degrees 1, 2 and 5 so they sit over major and minor, then pitched to the
  music's key (`Sfx.Prize.cs`, `Sounds.KeyRatio`). That idea is worth spreading (finding 9).
- **Ducking that knows what matters:** negations, big damage and the ending dip the music to 35% and bring it back
  over half a second; prizes dip it to 70%.
- **Repetition guards:** the same sound can't stack within 50 ms, repeats within 0.6 s get quieter.
- **Sounds timed to animations** (`BoardFx.Sound` schedules them with the tween), and silent while fast-forwarding.
- **The rain bed** for the Back Alley: looped filters so it has no click, a seam check in the harness.
- **Respect for settings:** nothing is built while muted or with music off; Reduced motion slows music changes to two
  seconds; Reactive music off keeps it steady.
- **Testability:** `--export-sounds` with `tour.wav` and per-boss demo duels with a timing guide; `Sounds.Played`
  counters, `DebugMusic`, `DebugBed`; `-musiccheck` covers layer states, the sting, endings, muted builds; `-menucheck`
  measures the listener's output level; the deck editor logs each sound for its checks. Most changes below can be
  guarded the same way.

## 7. A listening checklist for the owner (about 15 minutes, speakers then headphones)

1. **Settings → Sound effects**, click through to Off, then Interface sounds at 100%, and click any button. *Expect
   silence (finding 5).* Then click from 100%: does the jump to Off surprise you?
2. **Main menu, sit for a minute.** Do you notice the loop restarting about every 23 s? Open the deck editor, add a
   few cards, back out and in: does the music start over each time?
3. **Deck editor, add, remove, undo, redo, try a 4th copy.** Are the change sounds clear but quiet enough to repeat
   200 times? Is the refusal buzz too harsh?
4. **Free duel, Start.** The coin toss: does silence feel flat?
5. **First turns:** the turn chime and the phase banners. Would a soft tick on phase changes help or clutter?
6. **Normal Summon, then a Special Summon, over the everyday music.** Do the C major and D major chords sit with the
   E minor music, or rub?
7. **Build a 3-link chain** (the chain tutorial scene works). Does it feel like it builds? Are later links quieter?
8. **Destroy a monster, then banish one, then send one to the GY by an effect.** Can you tell them apart by ear?
9. **Lose a duel on the everyday theme.** Does the falling `lose` line clash with the music when it comes back?
10. **Pause mid-duel for 3 s and resume.** The menu loop comes in, then the duel theme starts over: does that bother you?
11. **Beginner Circuit's last match:** the sting on the versus screen, the theme, the tune arriving with the ace (mid-
    phrase?), drive at low LP, the heartbeat before a lethal attack, the victory ending. Exciting or tiring?
12. **Win with a big final blow with Sound effects and Music at 100%.** Any crackle or distortion?
13. **Open several packs** (the shop). Do Ultra and Secret pulls sound the same? Is `burst` too much after the 20th?
14. **On laptop speakers:** big damage, the ace summon, impact. Do they still hit? Compare to headphones.
15. **Switch to another app for a minute.** The music carries on: want it to stop? Then the headliners' show (sting,
    roar, groan) if you can reach it: never heard by anyone yet (TODO.md:346).

build/sounds`, then play `build/sounds/tour.wav` (every effect in a row) and `build/sounds/themes/demo-duel.wav` with

## 8. Owner decisions (recommendation first)

1. **More music: how.** *Recommended:* more synthesized themes in the existing system (M1, M2): no licence work, fits
   the sound the game has, about 5 agent-days, ears needed. *Or:* a human composer's score (B1), the biggest lift in
   production value but needs a person, terms and a week of integration. *Or:* CC0 library tracks: cheap, but each
   needs its source recorded and they rarely match each other.
2. **Pausing a duel.** *Recommended:* the duel theme carries on, dipped to 50% (Q2). *Or:* the music pauses with the
   duel. Default if building starts: dip.
3. **Mute in the background.** *Recommended:* on by default, a Settings row to turn it off (Q3). Default if building
   starts: on.
4. **Volume controls.** *Recommended:* the mixer with Master and real sliders (M3); until then Q1 at least. *Or:* keep
   the cycling rows but add 10% steps and stop 100% wrapping to Off.
5. **Ilse's and Vera's music.** *Recommended:* an existing theme in a new key now (Q6), their own with M2. *Or:* leave
   them on the everyday theme and change the check. Default if building starts: `nocturne` +3 for Ilse, `rhea` -2 for
   Vera (to be heard).
6. **Tune effects to the music.** *Recommended:* the tonal ones (Q8 list), not the thuds and swishes. *Or:* leave
   them; it's taste.
7. **Character text blips (B2).** *Recommended:* later, after M1-M3; a strong charm win but a matter of taste per
   character. *Or:* never; text stays silent.
8. **What to build first.** *Recommended:* one spec, **"Audio pass 1": Q1-Q10 plus M3 and M6** (about 7.5 agent-days):
   it fixes every CONFIRMED fault, fills the silent moments and adds the checks that guard audio without ears. Then
   M1 + M2 (music variety) as "Audio pass 2" with a listening pass. *Or:* M1 + M2 first if repetition is what you hear
   most in item 2 of the checklist.
