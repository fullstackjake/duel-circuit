# Part 1: The circuits

## Starting out

Choose **Campaign** in the main menu. A new campaign starts at Gus's card shop.

1. **Pick a starter deck.** It becomes your first deck, and its cards become your collection.
2. **Pick a way to play** (see [Two ways to play](#two-ways-to-play)).
3. You get **1,000 DP** (Duel Points) to spend.
4. Gus offers you a guided first duel. Take it if you're new to the game.

| Starter deck | How it plays |
|---|---|
| **Legendary Dragons** | Lord of D. and The Flute of Summoning Dragon bring out Blue-Eyes. |
| **Warrior's Charge** | Gearfried the Swordmaster destroys a monster with every Equip. Marauding Captain and Command Knight protect it. |
| **Undead Legion** | Zombie World, Book of Life, Pyramid Turtle and Goblin Zombie. |
| **Mekk-Knight Invoked Evolved** | A modern deck with Mekk-Knights, Aleister and Invocation, and hand traps. Much stronger than the early circuits. |

New to Yu-Gi-Oh!? **Legendary Dragons** is marked **Best first deck**. **Mekk-Knight Invoked Evolved** is strong from
the first duel, but its plays take practice.

## Two ways to play

You choose when you start a campaign:

| | **The shop's way** | **The history way: Historic Rules** |
|---|---|---|
| Ban lists | None for you: play your deck as it is | Each circuit's real TCG ban list from its era |
| Cards | Any card you own | Only cards that were out in that circuit's era |
| Good for | A first run (recommended) | A challenge, the way the game was played then |

In both modes, the CPU duelists (the computer players) play their decks exactly as they were built in their day.

**Historic Rules, the details:**

| Band | Ban list |
|---|---|
| Beginner | None |
| Novice | March 2011 |
| Intermediate | April 2015 |
| Advanced | October 2019 |
| Expert | June 2023 |
| Master | September 2026 (the last Master circuit: October 2026) |
| Gauntlet | September 2026 |

- The circuit shows its list. The **Duel** button names any card in your deck that breaks it.
- **Ban Passes** let you play one copy of a card the ban list doesn't allow. You start with 3. You get a 4th for
  clearing the Advanced band and a 5th for the Expert band. You can't buy them.
- Under Historic Rules, the three classic starter decks come in a 2011 version that the Novice list allows.
- Mekk-Knight Invoked Evolved is too new for Historic Rules after the Beginner band.

## The circuit map

The campaign is a map you climb: **22 circuits in 7 bands**, from the local card shop at the bottom to the Tier 0
Gauntlet at the top.

| Band | Circuits |
|---|---|
| **Beginner** | Beginner Circuit, Rookie Cup, Town Tournament, Regional Qualifier |
| **Novice** | Underdog Open, Trap Masters Cup, Small Engines Invitational |
| **Intermediate** | Synchro Dawn Classic, Xyz and Link Regionals, Link Age Open |
| **Advanced** | Golden Age Championship, Control Kings Cup, Hybrid Engines Invitational |
| **Expert** | Expert Series: Shadows and Storms, Rituals and Fire, The New Wave, Fall Format |
| **Master** | Master League: Link Wars, Graveyard Kings, Houston 2026, October 2026 |
| **Gauntlet** | Tier 0 Gauntlet |

**How it works:**
- Each circuit has three or four **matches**: a duelist with a particular deck.
- Win every match to clear the circuit. That pays its prize and opens the next one.
- Each circuit is a round node with its boss's face: dark with a padlock (locked), a pulsing halo (next up) or a crown
  (cleared). Click a node to see its matches.
- Tag Cups branch off the circuit that opens them.
- Each band opens newer sets in the Card Shop.
- The duelists get stronger band by band. The bands follow how strong the decks are, not the year they're from.

**DP for each duel:**
- A win pays DP. Later circuits pay more.
- The first time you win a match, you get 200 DP more.
- A loss pays 50 DP and a draw 100 DP, so you always earn something.

## Before and after a duel

**Before:** pressing **Duel** opens a versus screen. You see:
- you and your opponent side by side,
- three cards that show off each deck,
- their deck's year and play style, and your record against them,
- a **Scout** button for a scouting report (see [Stuck?](#stuck-scouting-advice-and-loaner-decks)).

**After:** a results screen shows:
- your opponent's reaction,
- the DP and EXP (experience points) you earned,
- your progress in the circuit,
- in gold, anything that just opened: the next circuit, new shop sets, a new ban list ahead.

From there: **Continue**, **Rematch**, or go to the **Card Shop**.

## The story

Short scenes play at key moments: starting out, clearing each band, each Tag Cup, your first tag duel with a partner,
some level milestones, becoming champion, and New Game+. There are over 180 story scenes.

The main cast:
- **Old Gus**, the shop's owner and your mentor.
- **Vera**, your rival, and later a partner.
- **Professor Hollis**, who explains each era as you reach it.

Each scene plays once. Click, or press **Space** or **Enter**, for the next line. **Skip** ends this scene and any
others waiting to play.

## Gus's Lessons

New to chains? Open **Lessons** at the top of the campaign screen. The number on the button shows how many you haven't
passed yet.

| Lesson | What you learn |
|---|---|
| **Your first duel** | The board, the phases, and answering a card |
| **1. Last in, first out** | A chain of three resolves backwards |
| **2. Catch the End Phase** | Using a set card at the end of your opponent's turn, by switching the **Chain** pill from AUTO to ON |
| **3. Chain to your own card** | Answering your own Dark Hole to save your Blue-Eyes |

Lessons always start with the **Chain** pill on AUTO, whatever your Settings say.

Pass the three chain lessons (1 to 3) to unlock a [special guest](#special-guests).

## Your decks in the campaign

In the campaign you build decks from **the cards you own**. Use **New deck** and **Edit deck** on the campaign screen.

- The search shows only your cards, and a deck can't use more copies than you have.
- You can own up to 3 copies of a card.

**Help with building:**

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Auto-fix** | Removes every card the ban list (or your collection) doesn't allow, and fills the gaps from your best spare cards |
| **Build** | Builds a whole deck from your collection, around the selected card if you own it |
| **Recipe** | Builds a known deck you own most of, and fills the gaps. Click again for the next one. |

Each change can be undone.

## Stuck? Scouting, advice and loaner decks

**Scouting reports.** Click **Scout** on the versus screen, or read one after a loss. A report tells you the deck's
plan, its key cards, what beats it, cards you could build with (and how many you own), and whether to go first or second.

**Stuck?** Lose the same match three times in a row. The button becomes **Stuck? Scout**, and the advice opens by
itself. It shows:
- which deck won most often when we tested this match, with rough odds ("about 7 in 10"),
- the next-best option,
- whether to go first or second,
- a stronger deck you can borrow, or a partner.

**Loaner decks.** You can borrow any deck you've unlocked in the [Recipe Book](#the-recipe-book): use the arrows under
your deck. A loaner is adjusted to the ban list, and you can't edit it. A win with a loaner pays half DP.

**Make it yours.** When the advice's best option is a loaner, it can offer to buy you that deck: **Make it yours · N DP**
buys the cards you're missing and saves the deck as yours. Then wins pay full DP, and you can edit it. Click once,
then click again to confirm.

# Part 2: Cards and DP

## The Card Shop

Open the **Card Shop** from the campaign screen.

**Booster packs**
- Packs from real TCG sets. You can search for a set.
- A pack has 9 cards: 7 Commons, 1 Rare or Super Rare, and 1 foil slot that can hold anything rarer.
- Pull rates are a little kinder than the real sets: about 1 pack in 3 or 4 has an Ultra Rare, and 1 in 7 a Secret
  Rare.
- Prices go by the set's year: from 150 DP for the oldest sets to 600 DP for the newest. Sets with no Commons cost more.
- Cards come face down. Click each one, or **Reveal all**. Rare cards glow and burst as they flip.
- New sets open as you clear bands.

**Booster boxes.** The 3 or 4 sets at the top of the shop, under **FOR THE** and your circuit's name, hold cards your
current opponents play. These sets also come in boxes: 24 packs for 20% less. Every box has at least one card of the
set's best rarity, up to Secret Rare. The shop shows which rarity.

**Structure Decks:** 8 ready-to-play decks. Each goes on sale when the shop reaches its year. Buying one adds its cards
to your collection and saves it as one of your decks.

| Structure Deck | Year | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Fury from the Deep | 2005 | 1,200 DP |
| Spellcaster's Command | 2005 | 1,200 DP |
| Machine Re-Volt | 2008 | 1,500 DP |
| Dinosaur's Rage | 2009 | 1,500 DP |
| Lost Sanctuary | 2011 | 1,800 DP |
| Dragunity Legion | 2013 | 2,000 DP |
| Synchron Extreme | 2015 | 2,200 DP |
| Mekk-Knight Crusade | 2018 | 2,500 DP |

**Your wishlist.** Cards you're missing for a recipe can go on a wishlist. In the shop, the wishlist button sorts sets
by how many of those cards each pack might give you.

**A 4th copy?** You keep at most 3 copies of a card. When a 4th arrives, the least valuable copy is sold for DP. If
the new copy is the better one, it says **UPGRADE**.

## Every copy is its own card

Each copy you own is different:

- **Rarity:** the printing you pulled. Your cards are drawn at your best copy's rarity everywhere.
- **Grade:** from 1 to 10, set the moment you get it. Most pack cards are Mint or Near Mint. Cards you buy, trade for
  or win as rewards are Near Mint. Until a card is graded, you see only its word, not its number.
- **Wear:** copies at Near Mint or below show it. A soft corner at Near Mint; nicked edges and first scratches at
  Excellent; dull gloss, dents and fading when Played; creases, chips and stains when Damaged. Each copy always looks
  the same.

| Grade | Word |
|---|---|
| 10 | Gem Mint |
| 9 | Mint |
| 7 to 8 | Near Mint |
| 5 to 6 | Excellent |
| 3 to 4 | Played |
| 1 to 2 | Damaged |

The condition is always written in words too, so you don't need to read the picture. If you'd rather see clean cards,
set **Card grades** to **Label only** in Settings.

**Value.** Every copy has a value in DP. It depends on the card, its rarity and its grade. A rare, perfect copy can be
worth a lot.

**Prized moments.** Your best copies sparkle and chime when they land face-up in a duel. For example, a Gem Mint
Secret Rare in a slab (a graded card sealed in a glass case).

## The Grading Desk

In the Card Shop, open the **Grading Desk**. Odile Varga runs it.

**Grade** a copy:
1. Pick a copy and pay the fee. The fee goes by the card's value (100 to 2,500 DP).
2. **Standard** takes 3 duels, and you can't use that copy meanwhile. **Express** costs double and is done right away.
3. It comes back in a **slab** (a sealed glass case) with its grade. Most of the time that's its true grade; sometimes one higher or
   one lower. The odds are on screen.
4. You can crack a slab open for free and send it again. It never comes back lower.

**Sell** a copy for a quarter of its value (never more than half of what you paid). You can't sell gifts, copies your
saved decks need, or cards you won in the Back Alley (unless you paid to grade them).

**Used** cards: 24 second-hand copies of real printings, some of them slabs. New stock arrives every 5 duels, with up
to 4 cards from your wishlist. **Special orders** get you a particular printing.

## Levels, EXP and Card Tokens

Every campaign duel earns **EXP** (experience points): more for wins, harder circuits and first wins, a little for losses.

| You get | When |
|---|---|
| DP | Every level |
| 1 Card Token | Every 2nd level, plus an extra one every 5th level |
| 1 Card Token | The first time you clear a circuit (3 in the Expert, Master and Gauntlet bands) |
| 1 Card Token | Each Tag Cup you win |

## The Card Trader

Open the **Card Trader** from the Card Shop. Card Tokens buy one copy of almost any card, from any era.

| If the card's lowest rarity is… | Price |
|---|---|
| Common or Rare | 1 token |
| Super or Ultra Rare | 2 tokens |
| Secret Rare or rarer | 3 tokens |

- With an empty search, the trader shows the cards missing from your wishlist first.
- **Buy a token:** 1,000 DP for the first, then 500 DP more each time.
- The 3-copy limit still applies. Under Historic Rules, tokens only buy cards that were out in the shop's current era.

## The Recipe Book

Open the **Recipe Book** on the campaign screen. It lists every deck the campaign's duelists play: 82 recipes, grouped
by map region. (These are different from the 74 historic tournament decks.)

- **Beat a duelist** while they play a deck to unlock its recipe. The results screen tells you.
- **A locked recipe** tells you who to beat, and where.
- **An unlocked recipe** shows:
  - the deck list, and its story (its era, how it worked, why it mattered),
  - how much of it you own, and where to get what you're missing,
  - **Add missing to wishlist**,
  - **Build this deck**, which builds it from your collection and opens it in the editor,
  - **Watch [name] play it** (the button shows the duelist's name): a short replay of the deck's owner at their best
    turn.
- **Recipe packs:** a pack of 9 cards from that recipe, missing cards first. Price goes by the band where you meet the
  deck: from 300 DP (Beginner) to 2,500 DP (Master and Gauntlet).
- **Buy what's missing:** every missing card at once, at its lowest rarity. For decks from later bands there's an
  extra fee on top, so it costs more than the cards alone.

A deck built from a recipe remembers it, so it can be [borrowed as a loaner](#stuck-scouting-advice-and-loaner-decks).

# Part 3: The people

## Bonds

Every win against a duelist grows your bond with them, anywhere in the campaign.

| Wins | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | They give you their signature card. |
| **More wins** | Sometimes a spare card from their deck (first playthrough only). |
| **3** | They'll partner you with any deck you've beaten them with. They offer you one card trade. |
| **5** | They give you their signature card again, at its rarest printing. |

**The card trade.** On a Tag Cup's page, pick that duelist as your partner. A **Trade** button appears beside your
bond. You give one spare card worth at least as many Card Tokens, and take one card from their decks.

**Partners.** Anyone you've beaten can partner you in the [Tag Cups](#tag-cups). **Kit, the Shop Regular**, can
partner you from the start, playing one of your other decks or a recipe.

## Bounties

Open **Bounties** on the campaign screen. The button appears after you clear the Beginner Circuit.

- There are **18 bounties**: three for each band (the Master band and the Gauntlet share one board).
- Each is a challenge to finish in a one-on-one circuit duel in that band. For example:
  - Finish a duel with a Normal Monster's attack.
  - Win after falling to 1,000 LP (Life Points) or less.
  - Deal 8,000 damage in one turn.
  - Win by turn 4.
- Each bounty pays once: the card your recipe is missing most. Your recipe is the one you put on your wishlist with
  **Add missing to wishlist** in the [Recipe Book](#the-recipe-book).
- With no recipe on your wishlist, the first bounty on each band's board pays a Card Token. The others wait, and pay
  once you pick a recipe.

## Tag Cups

Two against two, with a partner (see [Tag duels](PLAYING.md#tag-duels) for the rules). There are **7 Tag Cups**. Each
sits under the circuit that opens it. You don't need them to move on: play them when you like.

| Tag Cup | Opens after you clear |
|---|---|
| Card Shop Tag Night | Rookie Cup |
| Novice Tag Cup | Underdog Open |
| Intermediate Tag Cup | Synchro Dawn Classic |
| Advanced Tag Cup | Golden Age Championship |
| Expert Tag Cup | Expert Series: Shadows and Storms |
| Fall Format Tag Cup | Expert Series: Fall Format |
| Master Tag Cup | Master League: Graveyard Kings |

- Each cup has 3 tag matches against pairs of duelists from its band.
- Pick your partner on the cup's page with **‹ ›**. The game remembers your pick.
- A cup pays DP and EXP like its band's circuits, plus its prize and a Card Token the first time you win it.

## Special guests

Open **Guests** on the campaign screen. Guests are duelists outside the circuits. Each one unlocks with a milestone;
until then they show as "???".

| Guest | How to unlock | First-win prize |
|---|---|---|
| **Ines**, Gus's old rival | Pass Gus's three chain lessons | 1,500 DP |
| **Nocturne**, the Masked Challenger | Clear the Novice band | 3,000 DP |
| **Rhea**, the Tag Champion | Win 12 tag duels | 5,000 DP |
| **Seraph**, the Traveling Pro | Clear the Advanced band | 8,000 DP |
| **Duchess Ilse** | Win the Format Library's Every Era final | 10,000 DP |
| **Vera** (a rematch) | Win the Format Library's Every Era final | 10,000 DP |

- Click **Duel** to face a guest alone, or **Tag duel** to face them with their partner (Ines, Nocturne, Rhea and Seraph).
- Duchess Ilse plays four decks, one after another: beat one to face the next.
- Once you've beaten a guest, they can partner you in the Tag Cups.

## The Locker

Open the **Locker** on the campaign screen to hang a poster on your duelist panel. The button shows how many new
posters you have.

You get posters for:
- starting out (Shop Bell),
- clearing the Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced and Master bands, the Novice Tag Cup and the Tier 0 Gauntlet,
- winning the Every Era final,
- reaching the top of the Champion's Ladder,
- owning every card in a shop set,
- big moments in the Back Alley and its brackets.

You can also hang a gold emblem instead.

## New Game+

Click **New game** on the campaign screen. You get two choices; each is explained when you point at it, and needs a
second click to confirm.

- **New Game+:** the circuits start over. You keep your cards, decks, DP, level and tokens. Each new cycle is counted
  (NG+1, NG+2...).
- **Start over:** a fresh campaign. Your old save is kept as a dated backup in the `save` folder inside your `mods`
  folder.

# Part 4: After the Gauntlet

Clear the **Tier 0 Gauntlet** to become champion. That's not the end: new places open, one after another.

| What opens | When | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Format Library** | You clear the Tier 0 Gauntlet | A **Library** tab next to **Circuits** |
| **Back Alley** | You clear your first Library cup | A **Back Alley** tab |
| **Brackets** | You clear a Library cup or an Alley table | On the cup's page, and Baz's chalkboard in the Alley |
| **Champion's Ladder** | You win the Every Era final and win one duel against Duchess Ilse | A plaque on the Library shelf |

## The Format Library

Famous formats from Yu-Gi-Oh! history, each as a cup. **All 12 cups are open at once**: play them in any order.

1. Goat
2. TeleDAD
3. Synchro Peak
4. Rabbits & Rulers
5. HAT & Shaddoll
6. Nekroz & PePe
7. ABC to SPYRAL
8. Link Dawn
9. TOSS & Eldlich
10. Tearlaments
11. Kashtira & Snake-Eye
12. Ryzeal

**The rules:**
- Each cup uses its format's ban list and card pool (only cards that were out then), whichever way you play the campaign.
- Each cup has 4 matches. The Ryzeal cup has 3, plus an optional Champion's table.
- You can use Ban Passes and loaner decks. A win with a loaner pays half DP.

**Rewards:**
- 800 DP for the first win of each match; 150 DP after that.
- 2,500 DP and 2 Card Tokens for each cup you clear.
- **Format Master stamp** (+1 Card Token): win every match in a cup with a deck you built, with no loaner and no Ban Passes.
- A cleared cup sells its era's duel mat.

### The Every Era final

Clear all 12 cups to open it. Six rounds in a row, each in a different format, against that format's champion.

- Each round uses its cup's ban list and card pool.
- Your own decks only: no loaners and no Ban Passes.
- Lose a round and you replay it.
- Win to earn 10,000 DP, 3 Card Tokens and a poster. Two [special guests](#special-guests) arrive too.

## The Back Alley

Duels where you bet DP or a card, away from the shop. Alley duels pay **no DP**: you put something on the line, and
**you win your opponent's card**.

**The tables** open one after another. Most tables have 4 opponents a night (Orvel's Table has one). Beat them in order.

| Table | Opens | Your bet | What you'll face |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dumpster Row** | With the Alley | 300 DP | Old-school decks with banned cards mixed in |
| **The Basement Game** | After Dumpster Row | 1,000 DP | Mostly Synchro and early Xyz decks (2008 to 2014) |
| **The Black Market** | After The Basement Game | 1,000 DP | Decks from 2014 to 2018, with banned cards up to 2019 |
| **Orvel's Table** | After The Black Market | 3,000 DP and a card worth 1,000+ | One seat, against Patron Orvel. His prize is a slab. |
| **Nyx's Table** | After The Black Market | 1,000 DP | Ends with Nyx's own deck |

- You get your DP bet back if you win or draw. You also get it back if your opponent goes first and beats you
  before your first turn. If you lose or quit, you lose your bet.
- The first time you clear a table, you get a Card Token.
- Lose at Orvel's Table and your card goes to Moth's van for 5 duels. You can buy it back there.
- No duel goes on forever. A duel ends after turn 40. The duelist with more LP wins (equal LP is a draw).

**The Alley's house rules** ("The Alley" list):
- Forbidden cards are allowed, but only 1 copy each.
- 35 lock and loop cards are banned completely, for example Imperial Order, Royal Oppression and Self-Destruct Button.
- No Ban Passes. Old cards play with their original printed text.
- **Warrior's Charge players (the shop's way only):** your starter has 3 Reinforcement of the Army, but the Alley allows
  1. Edit the deck first. (Under Historic Rules your starter is the 2011 version, which has 1.)

**Headliners.** Some seats hold headliners: duelists famous for one big combo. Stop the combo before its payoff, then
win, to earn their Combo Breaker poster. (A rematch from the **Encore** button doesn't count.) Two Back Alley opponents
aren't in the game yet; another duelist sits in their seat for now.

**Moth's van.** Find **Moth's van** on the Alley map. It opens "Under the Counter":

| Tab | What's there |
|---|---|
| **Shelf** | 6 graded slabs of banned cards. New stock every 10 Alley duels. |
| **Used & graded** | 18 second-hand cards. New stock every 5 duels. |
| **Sell** | Sell cards like at the shop's sell desk. Moth won't buy cards you won in the Alley (unless you paid to grade them). |

**Your reputation.** Baz keeps a chalkboard. As you clear tables and win the Midnight Open, he gives you a new nickname.

**The Unlisted binder.** The contraband decks (decks with banned cards) you've beaten are kept in a binder on the
Library shelf.

## Brackets

Knockout tournaments against a field of CPU duelists: win and you go to the next round, lose and you're out.

**Where to find them:**

| Bracket | How to enter | Opens when you clear |
|---|---|---|
| **Library Locals** | **Locals** button on a Library cup | That cup |
| **Alley Brawl** | Baz's chalkboard in the Alley | Dumpster Row |
| **Midnight Open** | Baz's chalkboard in the Alley | The Black Market |

**What they cost and pay:**

| Bracket | Players | Entry fee | Prizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Library Locals** | 8 | 1,000 DP | Up to 900 DP and Prize Packs |
| **Alley Brawl** | 8 | 1,000 DP | Up to 900 DP and Contraband Packs |
| **Midnight Open** | 16 (the final is best of 3) | 2,500 DP | Up to 1,500 DP, Contraband Packs, and a Card Token for your first title |

- At Library Locals, using a loaner halves your prize.
- Brackets have no bets: you only pay the entry fee.
- A **Contraband Pack** has 5 cards: 4 loose cards and one sealed slab.

## The Champion's Ladder

The last climb. Open it from the plaque on the Library shelf. It opens after you win the Every Era final and win one
duel against Duchess Ilse.

**How it works:**
- 10 steps to the top. The game calls them rungs. Each is held by a strong duelist. Beat the one above you to take
  their rung.
- Lose and you keep your rung. You never fall.
- Every holder plays at full strength.
- Rules: the Tier 0 Gauntlet's ban list. Your own decks only, no loaners.
- On the 9th rung, Duchess Ilse plays all four of her decks in one sitting. Lose or draw and you start her rung again.
- Someone waits at the top. You'll see who when you get there.

**Rewards:**
- 800 DP the first time you climb each rung; 150 DP for a rematch.
- The top rung pays 5,000 DP, 3 Card Tokens and a poster.

## Coming later: No Limits

The Back Alley has one more table, **No Limits**, and a tournament, the **No Limits Invitational**. Both show
**Coming soon** and can't be played yet. They'll arrive in a later version.
